This guide shows what a CMI Level 5 assignment contains and how an assessor reads it. It is an annotated structure, not a student's text: each section is explained with its purpose, a word budget, and the evidence the assessor looks for. The worked outline uses Unit 501 Principles of Management and Leadership, the most requested Level 5 unit and usually the first management report a Level 5 student writes.

Most Level 5 units are assessed through a management report. A typical report runs to 3,000 to 4,000 words and cites a minimum of 10 to 12 sources. Training providers set their own word counts, so always check the assessment criteria and word count in your own assignment brief.

## What a CMI Level 5 Assignment Looks Like

| Feature | Level 5 expectation |
|---|---|
| Format | Management report with an executive summary, analysis sections, conclusion and recommendations |
| Scope | A team, department or operational function, set in a defined organisation (real or hypothetical) |
| Word count | Typically 3,000 to 4,000 words per unit, as specified by the provider |
| Sources | A minimum of 10 to 12, including CMI's ManagementDirect and academic management texts |
| Defining command verbs | Evaluate and Justify. Critically Evaluate in some units |
| Recommendations | Two or three SMART, evidence-based recommendations tied to the analysis |

The report format is the biggest change for students coming from Level 3 or Level 4, where essays are more common. The [guide to structuring a CMI assignment](/guides/cmi-assignment-structure/) covers the format in general; this page shows it applied to one unit.

## Annotated Structure of a CMI Level 5 Management Report (Unit 501)

The table below gives a suggested word budget for a 3,500-word report that includes the executive summary in the count. If your provider excludes it, add it on top. Adjust all figures to your brief.

| Section | Words | What the assessor looks for |
|---|---|---|
| Executive summary | 275 | A standalone summary, written last. A reader should learn which leadership approach is recommended and why without opening the report |
| Introduction | 300 | The organisation (real or hypothetical), the management context, the aims and the scope |
| AC1: Management and leadership | 550 | An analysis of the relationship between the two, using Kotter and Mintzberg. The relationship, not two separate definitions |
| AC2: Theories, models and styles | 1,100 | An Evaluate response for at least three theories, each against defined criteria, each ending in a conclusion |
| AC3: Applying theory to performance | 650 | The AC2 evaluation connected to a named management challenge and a named performance outcome |
| Conclusion | 250 | A summary of the analytical outcomes with no new information |
| Recommendations | 375 | Two or three SMART recommendations tied directly to the AC2 and AC3 analysis |
| Bibliography | Not counted | 10 to 12 Harvard references minimum |

AC2 is the highest-weighted criterion, so it takes the largest share. Three theories in 1,100 words leaves about 360 words each, which is only enough if every paragraph is doing evaluative work.

## How Evaluate Works Inside Each Section

Evaluate is the central verb at Level 5, and the most common place to lose marks. For each theory in AC2, the same five-step pattern keeps the section evaluative.

1. **State the criteria first.** Choose measures such as team motivation, performance impact and adaptability before the theory is discussed.
2. **Summarise the theory in a sentence or two.** Only as much description as the evaluation needs.
3. **Measure it against each criterion.** Use evidence from the organisation and the literature.
4. **Write the judgement.** Say how well the theory performs for this context.
5. **Compare it with the next theory.** Finish the section by stating which approach is more effective here, and why.

A list of pros and cons is not Evaluate. It is description with labels. The [CMI command verbs guide](/guides/cmi-command-verbs-explained/) shows how Evaluate differs from Analyse, Justify and Critically Evaluate.

## Weaker and Stronger Approaches at Level 5

| Weaker approach | Stronger approach |
|---|---|
| Describes each theory, then lists advantages and disadvantages | Sets criteria first, measures each theory against them, and reaches a judgement |
| Covers one tradition, such as transformational and situational leadership only | Covers a range, such as transformational, situational, contingency and the Managerial Grid |
| Applies theory to the organisation in general terms | Names the theory, the management challenge and the performance outcome it would improve |
| Writes the executive summary as a reworded introduction | Writes it last as a standalone summary of findings and recommendations |
| Recommends generic management advice | Gives SMART recommendations tied to the AC2 and AC3 analysis |

The executive summary and the recommendations are the two sections most often missing or underdeveloped in Level 5 submissions, so they are worth budgeting for before the first draft.

## Merit and Distinction at Level 5

At **Merit**, every theory in AC2 gets a complete Evaluate response with criteria, evidence and a conclusion. The organisational context in AC3 is clearly defined, the theories are connected to a specific performance outcome, and the recommendations are SMART and tied to the analysis. Harvard referencing is accurate and the sources include ManagementDirect and academic management texts.

At **Distinction**, the report does all of that and also:

- names the limitations of each theory and supports them with evidence
- examines where theories contradict each other and resolves the contradiction for the chosen organisation
- commits to a defended position in the conclusion and defends it against the alternatives
- justifies each recommendation with both the management theory and the organisational context

The practical difference is critical engagement. Merit applies the right frameworks and reaches sensible conclusions. Distinction questions where those frameworks conflict or break down and produces its own position.

## Using This Structure for Other Level 5 Units

Level 5 has 25 units, and most use the same report layout: executive summary, introduction, one section per Assessment Criterion, conclusion, recommendations and bibliography. What changes is the theory and the command verbs. Units with planning tasks ask for a Develop a Plan response with SMART objectives, resources, timelines and accountability, and some use Critically Evaluate, so check the verbs in your brief.

## FAQ: CMI Level 5 Assignment Examples

**What does a CMI Level 5 assignment look like?**
A management report of typically 3,000 to 4,000 words, with an executive summary, an introduction, one section per Assessment Criterion, a conclusion, SMART recommendations and a bibliography of at least 10 to 12 sources.

**What is the difference between Evaluate and Justify at Level 5?**
Evaluate applies criteria to weigh how well a theory or approach works. Justify gives evidence-backed reasons for a chosen position or recommendation, showing which theory supports it and why it is preferable to the alternatives in the specific context.

**How long should the executive summary be?**
About 250 to 300 words. It is written last and must stand alone, so that a reader who sees only the summary understands the key findings and recommendations.

**How should I split the word count in a Level 5 report?**
A common split for a 3,500-word report is about 275 words for the executive summary, 300 for the introduction, 550 for AC1, 1,100 for AC2, 650 for AC3, 250 for the conclusion and 375 for recommendations. Adjust it to your brief.
