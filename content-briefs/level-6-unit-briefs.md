These 16 briefs define the content for each Level 6 unit page. They follow the structural pattern established in `src/content/pages/cmi-level-5-unit-501.md` and `src/content/pages/cmi-level-7-unit-701.md`. When built, unit pages will live at `/cmi-level-6/unit-[slug]/`.

**Level context for all L6 briefs**
- Primary format: advanced management paper or strategic analysis document
- Word count per unit: 4,000–5,000 words
- Command verbs: Critically Evaluate, Critically Analyse, Critically Assess — the defining feature of Level 6 (see [CMI command verbs guide](/guides/cmi-command-verbs-explained/))
- Harvard referencing: 12–15+ sources per unit, including academic journals and empirical research
- Perspective: senior management / director level throughout — not operational
- Parent hub page: [CMI Level 6 Assignment Help](/cmi-level-6-assignment-help/)
- Breadcrumb: Home → CMI Level 6 Assignment Help → [Unit page name]

**Critical note for all L6 pages:** Every page must prominently explain what "Critically Evaluate" requires above and beyond Level 5 Evaluate — this is the most common referral cause and the most valued page feature for this audience.

- **AC2:** Critically analyse approaches to talent management and development in a professional management context

### Key Theories and Frameworks
- **High-performance team models:** Hackman's team effectiveness model (real team, compelling direction, enabling structure, supportive context, expert coaching). Critically evaluate — what does the evidence show about which factors most reliably produce high performance?

- **Remote and hybrid team leadership:** Added complexity at senior management level — trust-based rather than oversight-based leadership, intentional communication design, psychological safety at distance.

**Word count:** 4,000–5,000 words
**Primary command verbs:** Critically Assess, Critically Evaluate

### Assessment Criteria
- **AC1:** Critically assess organisational culture models and their applicability in a senior management context
- **AC2:** Critically evaluate the senior leader's role in shaping, sustaining, or transforming organisational culture
- **AC3:** Evaluate the relationship between culture and organisational performance

### Key Theories and Frameworks
- **Schein's Three Levels of Culture (in depth):** At L6 depth, not just descriptive — critically assess how valid Schein's model is. What does it explain well? What does it miss? (Limitations: overemphasises founder/leader role, treats culture as monolithic, limited guidance on how to change basic assumptions.)
- **Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions:** Power distance, individualism/collectivism, masculinity/femininity, uncertainty avoidance, long-term orientation, indulgence. Use for cross-cultural management contexts. Critically assess — his methodology (IBM survey data, 1970s) and generalisability have been criticised.
- **Cultural web (Johnson and Scholes):** Artefacts, stories, symbols, power structures, control systems, rituals. Evaluate as a diagnostic tool for mapping current culture.
- **Culture and strategy alignment:** Culture eats strategy for breakfast (Drucker attribution). Critically evaluate — is this true? What does the evidence say about culture-strategy fit?
- **Culture change at senior leadership level:** Why culture change is the hardest senior leadership challenge — culture resists deliberate change, change must come through systems and symbols not just leadership behaviour.
- **Culture and performance evidence:** Research on culture-performance links (Kotter and Heskett, 1992). Critically evaluate the evidence — what does it show and what are its limitations?

- **Innovation culture:** Psychological safety (Edmondson), tolerance for failure, intrapreneurship programmes. Evaluate what evidence shows about building sustainable innovation cultures.

### Assessment Criteria
- **AC1:** Critically evaluate knowledge management frameworks and their strategic application
- **AC2:** Critically analyse approaches to capturing, sharing, and retaining organisational knowledge
- **AC3:** Evaluate the role of organisational learning in strategic performance

### Key Theories and Frameworks
- **Nonaka and Takeuchi's SECI Model:** Socialisation, Externalisation, Combination, Internalisation — the spiral of knowledge creation from tacit to explicit. Critically evaluate: the model is influential but critics argue it oversimplifies tacit knowledge and is difficult to operationalise.
- **Tacit vs explicit knowledge (Polanyi):** Explicit knowledge is codifiable and transferable; tacit knowledge (know-how, professional judgement) is embodied and difficult to transfer. Critically analyse the implications for knowledge management strategy.
- **Intellectual capital framework:** Human capital (skills and capabilities of people), structural capital (systems, databases, processes), relational capital (relationships with customers and partners). Evaluate how organisations manage and measure intellectual capital.
- **Communities of practice (Wenger):** Informal groups that develop shared knowledge through participation. Evaluate as a knowledge sharing mechanism — limitations include self-selection and resistance to formalisation.
- **Organisational learning (Argyris and Schön):** Single-loop learning (adjusting actions within existing assumptions) vs double-loop learning (questioning the assumptions themselves). Critically evaluate whether organisations are capable of genuine double-loop learning.
- **Knowledge retention risk:** Succession planning, documentation, mentoring, exit interviews. Evaluate when key individuals leave.
