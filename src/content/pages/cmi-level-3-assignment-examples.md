This guide shows what a CMI Level 3 assignment contains and how an assessor reads it. It is an annotated structure, not a student's text: each section is explained with its purpose, a word budget, and the evidence the assessor looks for. The worked outline uses Unit 301 Principles of Management and Leadership, the entry point to the Level 3 First Line Management qualification. The same approach applies to the other Level 3 units.

Level 3 assignments are usually a structured essay or a short management report of 1,500 to 2,500 words. They are less formal than the reports required at Level 5, but they still need a clear structure, academic language and Harvard referencing. Training providers set their own formats and word counts, so always check the assessment criteria and word count in your own assignment brief.

## What a CMI Level 3 Assignment Looks Like

| Feature | Level 3 expectation |
|---|---|
| Format | A structured essay or short management report, with headings mapped to each Assessment Criterion |
| Scope | The first-line manager's own team and workplace |
| Word count | Usually 1,500 to 2,500 words per unit. Diploma programmes may total 3,000 to 4,000 across linked tasks |
| Sources | At least three at Pass and five to eight at Merit and Distinction, with in-text citations and a full bibliography |
| Command verbs | Identify, Describe and Explain, with Analyse and occasionally Evaluate at the upper end |
| Executive summary | Not always required, but the structure must still be clear |

For many Level 3 students this is the first academic assignment they have written. The subject matter is rarely the difficulty. Structuring an essay, referencing for the first time, and matching theory to workplace examples are.

## Annotated Structure of a CMI Level 3 Essay (Unit 301)

The table below gives a suggested word budget for a 2,000-word essay. Adjust it to the word count in your brief.

| Section | Words | What the assessor looks for |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 200 | The topic, the three Assessment Criteria the essay will cover, and the workplace context |
| AC1: Principles of management and leadership | 500 | Identify the principles, then describe each in detail. Kotter's distinction between managing complexity and leading change, and the planning, organising, directing and controlling functions |
| AC2: Roles and responsibilities of a first-line manager | 500 | Explain, not list. Mintzberg's roles applied to a first-line position, showing how each cluster works in practice |
| AC3: Management styles and how they are applied | 600 | Describe each main style, then explain when each applies. Lewin, White and Lippitt's autocratic, democratic and laissez-faire styles with situational logic |
| Conclusion | 200 | A short synthesis of the three criteria with no new information |
| Bibliography | Not counted | Harvard references, at least three at Pass and five to eight at Merit and Distinction |

AC3 takes the largest share because it carries two tasks, Describe and Explain, and is where Merit and Distinction responses separate from Pass.

## How Identify, Describe and Explain Work Inside Each Section

The three verbs ask for different amounts of work, and each Level 3 criterion usually uses one or two of them. A section that matches its verb, and goes no further, is the target.

- **Identify** means state what something is. Name the principle, role or style and give a short pointer to why it matters.
- **Describe** means explain it in more detail. Give an account of what it is and what it looks like in practice.
- **Explain** means show why or how it works. Give a cause-and-effect account, not a restatement.

Within each section, the PEEL pattern keeps paragraphs on task:

1. **Point.** Open with the point the paragraph makes.
2. **Evidence.** Support it with a named theory or source, cited in text.
3. **Explanation.** Say what the evidence shows and why it matters.
4. **Link.** Connect it to the criterion or to the next paragraph.

## Weaker and Stronger Approaches at Level 3

| Weaker approach | Stronger approach |
|---|---|
| Writes Level 5 depth for a task that only asks to Describe or Explain | Matches the depth to the verb and spends the saved words on the criteria that need them |
| Lists theories and roles without explaining them | Explains how each one works and why it matters to a first-line manager |
| Uses no headings, or headings that do not match the criteria | Maps a heading to each Assessment Criterion |
| Describes the styles but not when to use them | Explains which style suits which situation, and why |
| Cites sources loosely or only at the end | Cites in text at the point of argument and ends with a full Harvard bibliography |

One of the most common Level 3 mistakes is over-complicating the response. An accurate answer at the wrong depth does not give the assessor what the command verb asks for.

## Pass, Merit and Distinction at Level 3

At **Pass**, each criterion is addressed with the right verb: the principles are identified and described, the roles are explained, and the styles are described and explained. The theories are named correctly, referenced in text, and supported by at least three sources.

At **Merit**, the explanations are developed rather than asserted. The reasoning connects cause and effect, examples come from the workplace, and five to eight sources support the argument.

At **Distinction**, the response goes further on application. In AC3, for example, it connects the choice of style to team maturity, task urgency and the characteristics of individual team members, showing that style selection is principled and not arbitrary.

## Using This Structure for Other Level 3 Units

Level 3 has 12 units, and the essay layout stays the same: an introduction, one section per Assessment Criterion, a conclusion and a bibliography. What changes is the theory and the verbs. Check your brief for the verbs each criterion uses, and keep the depth to what they ask.

## FAQ: CMI Level 3 Assignment Examples

**What does a CMI Level 3 assignment look like?**
A structured essay or short management report of usually 1,500 to 2,500 words, with an introduction, one section per Assessment Criterion, a conclusion and a Harvard bibliography.

**What is the difference between Identify, Describe and Explain?**
Identify means state what something is. Describe means explain it in more detail. Explain means show why or how it works, with a cause-and-effect account.

**How many sources does a Level 3 assignment need?**
At least three at Pass and five to eight at Merit and Distinction, cited in text and listed in a full Harvard bibliography.

**How should I split the word count in a Level 3 essay?**
A common split for a 2,000-word essay is about 200 words for the introduction, 500 for AC1, 500 for AC2, 600 for AC3 and 200 for the conclusion. Adjust it to your brief.
