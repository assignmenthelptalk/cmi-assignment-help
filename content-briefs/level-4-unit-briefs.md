These 11 briefs define the content for each Level 4 unit page. They follow the structural pattern established in `src/content/pages/cmi-level-5-unit-501.md`. When built, unit pages will live at `/cmi-level-4/unit-[slug]/`.

**Level context for all L4 briefs**
- Primary format: management report or structured analytical essay
- Word count per unit: 2,000–3,500 words
- Command verbs: Analyse, Evaluate, Assess (step up from L3's Describe/Explain)
- Harvard referencing: 8–10 sources minimum
- Academic sources: management textbooks, CMI publications, ManagementDirect, peer-reviewed journals
- Parent hub page: [CMI Level 4 Assignment Help](/cmi-level-4-assignment-help/)
- Breadcrumb: Home → CMI Level 4 Assignment Help → [Unit page name]
- The defining shift from Level 3: students must Analyse and Evaluate, not just Describe and Explain. This distinction should be emphasised on every unit page.

**Assignment format:** Management report or analytical essay
**Word count:** 2,000–2,500 words
**Primary command verbs:** Analyse, Evaluate

### Assessment Criteria
- **AC1:** Analyse the factors that influence managerial styles and behaviours in an organisation
- **AC2:** Analyse a range of management styles and their application within an organisational context
- **AC3:** Evaluate the impact of managerial styles and behaviours on organisational performance

### Key Theories and Frameworks
- **Situational Leadership (Hersey and Blanchard):** Four styles (Directing, Coaching, Supporting, Delegating) matched to follower development level (D1–D4). Core framework for AC2 — analyse each style and evaluate its application to different management situations.
- **Transformational and Transactional Leadership (Bass):** Describe the contrast, then Analyse which is more effective in different organisational contexts. Use in AC2 range.
- **Contingency Theory (Fiedler):** Leadership effectiveness depends on match between leader style and situational favourability. Analyse the factors that determine which style is appropriate (leader-member relations, task structure, position power).
- **Goleman's Emotional Intelligence:** Six leadership styles (Coercive, Authoritative, Affiliative, Democratic, Pacesetting, Coaching) — tied to emotional competencies. Use in AC1 to analyse individual factors influencing managerial behaviour.
- **Factors influencing managerial style:** Organisational culture, team maturity, task type, time pressure, sector. Use in AC1.

### Assessment Criteria
- **AC1:** Analyse the concept of organisational culture and its components
- **AC2:** Analyse how organisational culture shapes values and behaviour
- **AC3:** Evaluate the manager's role in influencing and developing organisational culture

### Key Theories and Frameworks
- **Schein's Three Levels of Culture:** Artefacts (visible structures and processes), Espoused Values (stated goals and philosophies), Basic Underlying Assumptions (unconscious beliefs). Core framework for AC1 — analyse each level and examine their relationship.
- **Handy's Four Culture Types:** Power culture, role culture, task culture, person culture. Analyse the characteristics of each type, their strengths and limitations, and which contexts suit each.

- **Cultural web (Johnson and Scholes):** Stories, symbols, power structures, organisational structure, control systems, rituals. Use in AC2 to analyse how culture manifests in observable behaviours.
- **Manager's role in culture:** Climate setting, modelling values, reinforcing norms. Evaluate in AC3 — including limitations of the manager's influence (culture change is systemic, not individual).

- **AC2:** Evaluate change management models and approaches

### Key Theories and Frameworks
- **Kotter's 8-Step Change Model:** (1) Create urgency, (2) Form a powerful coalition, (3) Create a vision for change, (4) Communicate the vision, (5) Remove obstacles, (6) Create short-term wins, (7) Build on the change, (8) Anchor the changes. - **Lewin's Force Field Analysis:** Driving forces vs restraining forces. Analyse the forces for and against a specific change initiative — evaluate this as a diagnostic tool for understanding change readiness.
- **ADKAR Model (Prosci):** Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement. Individual-level change model — evaluate as a complement to Kotter's organisational-level approach.
- **Lewin's Three-Stage Model:** Brief mention — Unfreeze-Change-Refreeze (covered at L3). At Level 4, move beyond description to evaluation of its applicability.
- **Resistance to change — analytical level:** Analyse the sources of resistance (loss of control, uncertainty, lack of trust, poor communication) and evaluate management strategies (involvement, communication, support, negotiation, coercion) with evidence on their effectiveness.
- **Change drivers:** PESTLE factors, technological disruption, mergers and acquisitions, regulatory change, cost reduction. Use in AC1.

### Assessment Criteria
- **AC1:** Analyse the recruitment and selection process within an organisational context
- **AC2:** Evaluate selection methods and their appropriateness for different roles
- **AC3:** Analyse the equality and diversity considerations in recruitment and selection

### Key Theories and Frameworks
- **Recruitment and selection process:** Workforce planning → job analysis → job description and person specification → advertising → shortlisting → selection → offer → induction. Analyse each stage and the manager's role at each point.
- **Job analysis methods:** Task analysis, competency analysis, observation, interviews with job holders. Use in AC1 to explain how requirements are established before advertising.
- **Selection methods:** Structured interview, competency-based interview (STAR method), aptitude testing, assessment centre, work sample test, personality profiling. Evaluate each method — reliability, validity, cost, candidate experience.
- **Equality Act 2010 in recruitment:** Protected characteristics, lawful vs unlawful pre-employment questions, equal opportunities monitoring, positive action (not positive discrimination). Analyse the manager's legal obligations in AC3.
- **Induction planning:** The purpose of structured induction, legal requirements (health and safety induction), and the retention benefit of effective onboarding.
